A letter from the person who made it

Dear reader,

For a long time my world lived in a folder. A document for each country, a spreadsheet of kings, a map with pencil all over the back of it, and a file called NAMES-FINAL-2 that was neither. Every time I sat down to write a chapter I spent the first half hour finding out what I had already decided.

←The folders are still on my desktop. I don’t open them.

Otto is what I built instead. You make an entry for each thing in your world (a person, a place, a faction, a language, a calendar, a battle, a ring) and the entries know about each other. Write that someone was born in Harrowgate, and Harrowgate’s page lists her among the people born there. You didn’t have to do that, and you never will.

←Harrowgate is made up for this page. The sample world that opens on first launch has its own.

Dates are kept on your world’s own calendars, with their own months and eras, and you can write “ten years after the Fall” and have it mean something. Maps are your own images with places pinned to them. And the book sits beside it all: chapters are entries too, written in a quiet full-screen writer and sent to Word, with proper headings, when you want a draft you can print.

It runs on your computer and nowhere else. There is no account. I don’t know who you are, and I would like to keep it that way.

Three promises

How it is built, in plain words, so you can decide whether to trust it with ten years of work

i.It runs on your machine and nowhere else.

There is no account to make and no server for Otto to talk to. Nothing is sent anywhere. It works with the network cable out, on a train, in a cottage with no signal. Your whole world is one SQLite file1 that you can copy, back up, or carry to another computer on a stick.

←Try it with the wifi switched off. Nothing changes.

ii.What you write stays yours, in a form you can read without me.

At any moment you can export the lot as plain JSON or Markdown: every entry, every link, every date. Before each update Otto sets a backup aside2, and every entry keeps its own version history, so last Tuesday’s draft of a chapter is still there. Updates change the program. They never touch what you wrote.

←Markdown opens in any text editor made in the last forty years, and will in the next forty.

iii.The furniture stays where it is.

Otto comes with its own kinds of thing (people, places, factions, languages, calendars, events, artifacts, ideas) and those are fixed. Whatever you add goes alongside them: a form for ships, a category for minor gods. No update will rename your additions or take them away. I won’t rearrange the room while you’re out of it.

  1. 1SQLite is the database format inside most phones and web browsers. It is open, thoroughly documented, and the Library of Congress lists it among its recommended formats for keeping data. ↩
  2. 2The backups rotate: when a new one is made the oldest goes, so they never quietly fill your disk. ↩
